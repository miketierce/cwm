# Lab Diary — 22 May 2026

## Session Goal

Bring up the v3 breadboard hardware stack (Board S / Board D / Board A / 16-channel relay mux) for DDS-driven acoustic experiments on a glass plate with PZT transducers. Diagnose and resolve end-to-end signal path issues.

---

## What We Did (Chronological)

### 1. Frequency Sweep — No Acoustic Signal Detected

Ran dual-DDS in-phase sweep from 4.5 kHz to 390 kHz with 50 FFT averages per frequency, comparing relay 7 ON (acoustic path) vs relay OFF (EMI-only baseline). All ratios ≈ 1.0 across the entire range — no acoustic signal found.

### 2. EMI Diagnosis — Mux Bus Wire as Antenna

Discovered that the 4567 Hz DDS tone appeared at **9.4 mV** on Board A output regardless of relay state (ON or OFF). This was EMI, not acoustic.

**Isolation test:** Disconnected the mux bus wire from Board A A20. Tone dropped from 9.4 mV to 0.58 mV — confirming the mux bus wire was acting as an antenna picking up radiation from the drive twisted pair.

**Fix:** Twisted the mux bus wire (relay COM → Board A A20) with an AGND return wire. EMI dropped from 9.4 mV to **0.21 mV** — a 45× reduction (−33 dB).

### 3. Relay Module Not Switching — JD-VCC Missing

Observed that relay module "Dx" LEDs lit (opto-isolator triggering) but "Rx" LEDs stayed off (relay coils not energizing). The relay contacts were never actually closing.

**Root cause:** The 16-channel opto-isolated relay module has a separate "5V" pin header that powers the relay coil driver transistors. This was unconnected. Only the screw terminal (powering the opto-isolator logic side) was wired.

**Fix:** Connected the "5V" header pins to the screw terminal's + side. Rx LEDs now light, relays click, contacts close.

**Impact:** Every "relay ON" measurement prior to this fix was actually measuring with an open relay. The relays had NEVER been switching this entire session (and likely since the v3 hardware was first assembled).

### 4. Tap Test — Acoustic Path Confirmed

With relays properly powered, ran a 20-second tap capture:

- **3 taps detected** at 1.5× baseline (1225–1243 mV vs 832 mV)
- Dominant frequencies from glass plate: **291 Hz, 388 Hz, 484 Hz**
- Confirms: glass → RX PZT → relay → Board A → scope path works

### 5. DDS Drive — Too Weak for Acoustic Coupling

Re-ran DDS sweep (both channels in-phase) at glass resonance frequencies (291, 388, 484 Hz) and buzzer PZT range (1–7 kHz). Relay ON vs OFF ratio remained ≈ 1.0 at all frequencies.

**Conclusion:** The DDS at ~670 mVpp through the 10 kΩ sum resistors does not produce sufficient mechanical displacement to detect through glass. The signal is below the system noise floor.

## Key Findings

### A. Relay Module Requires JD-VCC Connection

The 16-channel opto-isolated relay module has two power domains:
- **Screw terminal (DC+/DC−):** Powers opto-isolator LEDs (logic side)
- **5V header pins:** Powers relay coil drive transistors (output side)

Both must be connected for the relay contacts to actually switch. The Dx LED indicates input received; Rx LED indicates relay energized. If only Dx lights → JD-VCC side has no power.

### B. Twisted-Pair Mux Bus Eliminates EMI

The single-wire mux bus from relay COM to Board A A20 acted as an antenna, picking up 9.4 mV of DDS EMI. Twisting it with an AGND return wire reduced coupling to 0.21 mV (−33 dB). Essential for any sensitive measurement.

### C. DDS Drive Amplitude Is Insufficient

The AD9833 DDS outputs ~670 mVpp. Through the 10 kΩ sum resistors into a capacitive PZT load (~20 nF, Z ≈ 2 kΩ at 4 kHz), the PZT sees only ~112 mVpp. This produces sub-nanometer displacement — undetectable by the receive PZT + preamp.

**Comparison with v2 cassette (April 7–11 sessions):**
- v2 used the **PicoScope built-in AWG: 2 Vpp, 600 Ω source impedance**
- At 4 kHz into 20 nF PZT: ~1.54 Vpp at the PZT
- That's **14× more drive** than the current DDS setup
- Even at 2 Vpp, the AWG produced only 2–11× resonance ratios above feedthrough
- Current DDS provides ~14× less voltage → signal ~14× weaker → below noise

## Board D Buffer Amplifier: Build, Debug, and Stability (Phase 6–10)

With the DDS drive confirmed insufficient at ~670 mVpp, the session pivoted to building the gain-of-3 buffer amplifier on Board D per `companion/breadboard_layout_v3.html` (Wiring Guide v3.1). The buffer should boost the DDS signal to ~2 Vpp for adequate acoustic coupling through glass.

### Phase 6: OPA2134PA Installation and Initial Signal Path Trace

Installed the OPA2134PA DIP-8 at E10–E13 / F10–F13 (notch left). Wired per v3.1 spec:
- V+ (G10 → red +5V rail), V− (B13 → blue GND rail)
- Channel A parked: C10→C11 (out→−in), D12→D13 (+in→GND)
- Input: Board S a35 → Board D J5 (inter-board wire), jumper I5→I9, 100 nF coupling cap col 9→col 13
- Bias: 100 kΩ from col 13 to red +5V rail, 100 kΩ from col 13 to col 14
- Gain network: Rf (22 kΩ), Rg (10 kΩ col 12→col 15), series cap (10 µF col 15→col 14), GND (J14 → blue rail)
- Bypass: 100 nF ceramic col 10→col 14
- Output: R_OUT 47 Ω I11→I20, PZT at J20/A20

Systematic signal path verification using PicoScope + FFT at 4567 Hz:

| Probe Point | Measurement | Status |
|-------------|-------------|--------|
| Board S a35 (DDS sum node) | 273–363 mVpp, 65 mV @ 4567 Hz | ✓ |
| Board D col 5 (inter-board wire) | 675 mVpp, 62 mV @ 4567 Hz | ✓ |
| Board D col 9 (after jumper I5→I9) | 327 mVpp, 65 mV @ 4567 Hz | ✓ |
| Board D col 13 (+IN B, after C_IN) | 304 mVpp, 65 mV @ 4567 Hz | ✓ |
| Board D col 13 DC bias | 2090 mV | ✓ (expected ~2.5 V) |

**DDS protocol discovery:** Both F1 and F2 commands must be sent (with 1 s settle) for full analog output at the sum node. Sending only F1 produces weak or absent signal. DDS status query returns `DDS:1=4567,2=4567,3=0;P:1=0,2=0,3=0`.

### Phase 7: Unity Gain Diagnosis — Rf in Wrong Position

Signal reached the op-amp input perfectly but the output showed unity gain:

| Point | DC | AC @ 4567 Hz | Gain |
|-------|-----|-------------|------|
| Col 13 (+IN B) | 2090 mV | 65 mV | — |
| Col 11 (OUT B) | 2197 mV | 65 mV | **1.0×** |
| F20 (after R_OUT) | — | 2 mV | — |

Systematic isolation of the feedback path:

1. **Col 14 (GND node):** 0 mV DC ✓ — GND wire was loose, reseated in new pin
2. **Col 15 (Rg/series cap junction):** 2190 mV DC ✓ — Rg connected (DC blocked by cap)
3. **Col 15 AC with DDS:** 0 mV ✓ — series cap properly shunting AC to ground
4. **Conclusion:** Full Rg path intact (col 12 → Rg → col 15 → cap → col 14 → GND). The only explanation for unity gain with a working Rg is **Rf ≈ 0**.

**Root cause found:** Rf was installed at **col 12 → col 13** instead of the correct **col 11 → col 12**. This shorted −IN B (pin 6, col 12) directly to +IN B (pin 5, col 13), bypassing the output entirely and creating a unity follower. The output (pin 7, col 11) had no feedback path.

**Lesson:** On a breadboard with 2.54 mm column spacing, a 1-column error on a short resistor completely changes the circuit topology. The error was invisible to visual inspection and required isolating every node in the feedback chain to diagnose.

### Phase 8: Rf Corrected → Oscillation at 388 Hz

Moved Rf from col 12→13 to the correct col 11→12 (OUT B → −IN B).

**Immediate result:** Low-frequency oscillation.

| Measurement | Value |
|-------------|-------|
| DC level | 2061 mV (not saturated) |
| Oscillation frequency | **388 Hz** |
| Oscillation amplitude | **2685 mVpp** |
| 4567 Hz component | 26 mV (buried) |

The circuit was stable as a unity follower but oscillated the instant gain was applied. This is consistent with inadequate phase margin at the OPA2134PA's minimum supply voltage (+5V single-supply, spec minimum is ±2.5V = 5V total).

**Contributing factors:**
- **Marginal supply:** Arduino USB +5V (possibly 4.6 V through Schottky diode) is at the OPA2134PA's absolute minimum rated supply.
- **Long supply wires:** +5V travels from USB → Arduino regulator → Board S rail → inter-board wire → Board D rail → pin 8. Significant series inductance.
- **100 nF coupling cap + 50 kΩ bias parallel resistance:** High-pass corner at ~32 Hz. Below this, the input signal is attenuated but the feedback network still has full gain, creating low-frequency gain peaking that the loop cannot handle.
- **Breadboard parasitics:** Stray capacitance between adjacent columns adds phase shift.

### Phase 9: Oscillation Suppression Attempts

**Attempt 1 — 10 µF 63 V electrolytic bypass across J-side power rails:**
- Installed directly across +5V and GND rails near the chip.
- **Result:** Oscillation shifted from 388 Hz down to **97 Hz**. Amplitude reduced but not eliminated. Supply stiffening helped but didn't solve the fundamental stability problem.

**Attempt 2 — Replace coupling cap: 100 nF → 4.7 µF (ceramic, code 475):**
- Rationale: Raising C_IN from 100 nF to 4.7 µF lowers the high-pass corner from 32 Hz to 0.68 Hz, reducing the low-frequency gain peaking window.
- The 4.7 µF cap's lead spacing was too wide for col 9→col 13, so BNC pin segments were crimped onto the leads as extensions.
- **Result:** Oscillation stopped. Circuit appeared stable. But **0 mV at 4567 Hz reached col 13**. The 4.7 µF cap with crimped extensions was not reliably passing signal.

**Attempt 3 — 1 nF compensation cap (code 102) across Rf (J11→J12):**
- Intended to roll off gain above ~7 kHz for phase margin.
- **Result:** DC bias shifted from ~2100 mV to 1129 mV. AC signal still absent. Removed.

**Attempt 4 — Revert to 100 nF coupling cap:**
- Removed the 4.7 µF cap and restored the original 100 nF film cap.
- Reseated jumper I5→I9 and checked inter-board wire.
- **Result:** 97 Hz oscillation returned weakly (5 mV, 67 mVpp). No 4567 Hz signal detected at col 13 — the DDS had likely dropped out again (USB disconnection pattern).

### Phase 10: Signal Lost — Session End

Final probe at col 5 (inter-board wire landing): 0 mV at 4567 Hz. The DDS was responding to serial commands (`DDS1:4567`, `DDS2:4567`) but no analog signal was arriving at Board D. This matched a pattern observed 3+ times during the session: the DDS USB disconnects during rework, and even after reconnection, the analog output is sometimes absent.

## Recurring Issues Summary

### 1. DDS USB Disconnections
The DDS Arduino Nano USB cable (`/dev/cu.usbserial-1120`) disconnected 3+ times during the session. Each disconnect required replugging and the 2.5 s Arduino reset wait. Firmware always responded correctly after reconnection, but analog output was sometimes absent until both F1 and F2 were re-sent.

### 2. Breadboard Fragility
Every component swap dislodged at least one neighboring connection. Confirmed failures: jumper I5→I9 (twice), inter-board wire (once), GND at J14 (once). The col 9–15 region has 7+ components in 7 columns — too dense for reliable rework.

### 3. OPA2134PA Unstable at Minimum Supply
The OPA2134PA oscillates at gain > 1 on a single +5V supply. This is its absolute minimum rated voltage. The circuit is stable as a unity follower but immediately oscillates when Rf provides gain. The 10 µF electrolytic shifts the oscillation frequency but does not suppress it.

## Next Steps

1. **Verify DDS signal chain first** — Confirm signal at Board S a35 and Board D col 5 before any Board D changes.
2. **Reduce gain** — Swap to 10 kΩ Rf + 10 kΩ Rg (gain = 2×, output ~1.3 Vpp). Lower gain improves phase margin and may stabilize.
3. **Try a different op-amp** — A true single-supply rail-to-rail part (MCP6002, OPA340, LM358) would be more appropriate for +5V operation.
4. **Try ±9V batteries** — Two 9V batteries (as used on Board A) would give the OPA2134PA ±9V = 18V total supply, well within its comfort zone. Requires rewiring V+ and V−.
5. **Try 1 µF coupling cap** — Code 105 from the ceramic kit. Corner frequency ~3.2 Hz (vs 32 Hz for 100 nF), reducing low-frequency gain peaking without the lead-extension problems of the 4.7 µF.
6. **Consider clean rebuild** — The breadboard has been reworked extensively. A fresh build with verified component placement at each step would eliminate accumulated connection faults.
