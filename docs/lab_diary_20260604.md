### Rationale for Independent TX (not cascade)

The 25mm plate should be its **own independent TX circuit**, not cascaded with the 100mm plate. Reasons:

1. Clean null-test interpretation — no question about signal leaking from the big plate
2. Different frequency regime (~200 kHz – 2 MHz vs. 35–120 kHz)
3. Independent characterization of miniaturization scaling
4. No coupling confusion between plates

- GP3 (pin 5) → CH2 → 100mm plate NE PZT (or SW for 2-tone)

**New channel for 25mm plate:**

- GP5 (pin 7) → CH4 → 25mm plate SW PZT (primary TX)

### Wiring Plan

```
25mm plate circuit (completely isolated from 100mm plate):

Pico GP5 (pin 7) ──[220Ω]──→ 25mm SW PZT (+)
                              25mm SW PZT (−) ──→ Pico GND (pin 8 or 38)

25mm NW PZT (+) ──→ Board A preamp input (×11) ──→ PicoScope Ch A

25mm NE PZT (+) ──→ PicoScope Ch B (direct)

```

**Key isolation:** The 25mm plate TX (GP5) must NOT share the same 220Ω/GND path as the 100mm plate TX (GP2/3/4). Separate resistor, separate GND return wire.

### Alternative: SE Corner of 100mm Plate

The SE corner PZT on the 100mm plate is currently unused ("spare"). This could serve as a tape-on/tape-off null test PZT while keeping the superglued NW/NE/SW PZTs undisturbed. However:

- **Pro:** Same plate, same modes, known frequency range
- **Con:** Already has 3 PZTs loading the plate — adding/removing the 4th changes the loading and therefore Q/modes slightly
- **Decision:** Use BOTH. The 25mm plate gives a clean independent null test; the SE corner of the 100mm plate gives a same-plate repeatability check.

### Expected Frequency Range (25mm plate)

For a thin square plate, modes scale as:
$$f_{mn} \propto \frac{h}{L^2} \sqrt{\frac{E}{\rho(1-\nu^2)}}$$

With L*new/L_old = 25/100 = 0.25, and h unchanged (1mm):
$$f*{new} = f*{old} \times (100/25)^2 = f*{old} \times 16$$

Expected range: 35 kHz × 16 ≈ **560 kHz** to 120 kHz × 16 ≈ **1.9 MHz**

PicoScope 2204A supports up to 10 MS/s per channel (adequate for 2 MHz).
Pico NCO at 126 MHz clock: minimum half-period = 500 ns → max freq = 1 MHz (plenty for fundamental modes; higher modes need firmware modification for faster toggling or use odd harmonics of square wave).

**Note:** The 20mm PZTs are nearly as large as the 25mm plate (80% coverage). This high coupling ratio may give better drive efficiency but could also significantly load the plate. We'll see.

## Open Questions

1. Will masking tape provide adequate acoustic coupling at 500 kHz – 2 MHz? (Higher frequencies = shorter wavelength = more sensitive to coupling impedance. May need firmer pressure or different tape.)
2. At 80% coverage (20mm PZT on 25mm plate), does the PZT mass loading dominate the plate's own modes? PZT mass ≈ 3–4g vs. plate mass ≈ 1.4g — the PZT might be 3× heavier than the plate!
3. Should we use a smaller PZT (10mm disc) for less loading?

### PZT Mass Loading Concern

With 10mm PZTs (not 20mm), loading is much more reasonable:

- 25mm plate mass: ~1.375 g (fused silica, 2.2 g/cm³ × 0.625 cm³)
- 10mm PZT disc mass: ~0.3 g each (PZT-5A, ~7.8 g/cm³, 10mm × ~0.5mm = 0.039 cm³ × 7.8 ≈ 0.3g)
- With 4 PZTs: adds ~1.2 g to a 1.375 g plate (roughly doubling effective mass)
- Coverage: 10mm disc on 25mm plate ≈ 12.6% area per PZT (much better than 80% with 20mm!)

**Revised frequency estimate with 10mm PZT loading:**

- Effective mass ≈ plate + 4×PZT mass ≈ 1.375 + 1.2 = 2.575 g (1.87× heavier)
- Frequency scales as 1/√m for mass loading, combined with 1/L² geometric:
- f_new ≈ f_old × 16 / √(2.575/1.375) ≈ f_old × 16 / 1.37 ≈ f_old × 11.7
- **Revised range: ~420 kHz – 1.4 MHz**

This is well within PicoScope range (10 MS/s) and Pico NCO range (up to ~1 MHz easily at 126 MHz clock).

**Action:** Start with a wide sweep (100 kHz – 2 MHz) to find whatever modes exist.

### Root Cause: Masking Tape Does Not Couple Acoustically

Masking tape bonding provides insufficient mechanical impedance matching for PZT-to-glass acoustic transfer. The taped PZTs are electrically connected but acoustically deaf. This explains:
- Why the 25mm plate sweep showed only EMI, not real modes
- Why the null test showed no change when plate was removed
- Why the "172 kHz mode" had unstable amplitudes (EMI varies randomly)

### Revised Null Test Strategy: Tape → Superglue Comparison

Instead of "lift PZT to break coupling," we use a **bond quality comparison:**

1. **Baseline (captured today):** PZT taped in position, wires connected, relay active → measures ONLY EMI (proved: no acoustic component)
2. **After superglue:** Same PZT, same wires, same relay, same physical position → measures EMI + acoustic
3. **Difference = pure acoustic signal**

**Why this is methodologically superior:**
- Nothing electrical changes between conditions (same PZT, same wires, same relay)
- Reviewer cannot argue "you changed the wire routing" or "introduced a ground loop"
- The only variable is bond mechanical quality (tape vs. cyanoacrylate)
- Baseline data already captured and timestamped

**Plan:** Superglue the relay 6 PZT to Plate I SE corner. Re-run same modes. The signal that appears is unambiguously acoustic.
