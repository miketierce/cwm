The strongest honest case is not that the plate does anything new. It is that the project now holds three things other people can use: controlled negative results on a class of hardware about which positive claims are still being published; a catalogue, with data, of the specific artefacts that made a linear plate look like a memory and a computer; and a complete record of how LLM-assisted research went wrong and was pulled back.

(a) Negative-results and fair-baseline paper. As physics it is not new: theory says a linear reservoir with a linear readout creates no new features (Bollt 2021; Nokkala et al. 2026), and Wright et al. (2022) reported their plate as close to a linear classifier. As evidence it is useful. First-round reviewers found no no-substrate control in six MEMS reservoir papers, and I found a March 2026 preprint reporting a piezoelectric-ceramic reservoir 2.4 points above logistic regression on MNIST (abstract only). This project ran the missing controls in every October session (a no-plate loopback and a dummy receiver), and its own stored analysis shows a 32-dimension digital random projection scoring 87.7% against 88.0% for the 240-feature plate, and 99.5% at 240 dimensions. Venue: a journal that already carries reservoir-benchmark critique (International Journal of Parallel, Emergent and Distributed Systems) or an unconventional-computing journal; arXiv cs.ET or physics.app-ph if an endorser is found, otherwise Zenodo. Needed first: one preregistered series on the reworked fixture with third-party timestamps, a second plate, a plate thermometer, the temporal-memory test that was planned and never run (O6), baselines scripted with fixed seeds, and each artefact shown switched on and off.

(b) LLM methods case study. Partly new. Neighbours exist for machine-learning agents (Trehan and Chopra 2026; Beel et al. 2025) and for audit discipline in AI-assisted writing (Zhou and Yu 2026); in two searches I found no bench-hardware case. The material is unusually complete. I confirmed that v10 (5 March, 11:38) set predictions with kill criteria; that v11, committed 42 minutes later, already had a "Prediction | Measured" table; that a 6 March commit "corrected" the "ring-down" from 169 to 180 ms when a wave-speed constant changed; and that the first real rods (7 April) gave Q of 10 to 13 against v10's own kill line of 100. Q then went through 7,687 to 60,092 (April, fits with R-squared under 0.18), 2,275 (May, R-squared 0.077) and finally 136 to 635 (October, two methods agreeing). Venue: a metascience or data-science journal, or a long essay with a DOI. Needed first: the owner's own prose, an error table with counts and time-to-detection, and legal advice if a non-provisional is still contemplated, because the essay would state that the specification's measurements were not made.

(c) Instrument and dataset. Low novelty (an open resonant-ultrasound toolbox exists, and impedance-based structural monitoring uses the same glued-disc arrangement), but it is the foundation for everything else and nearly free. The October raw data (1.1 GB, hashed) and scripts reproduce exactly: I recomputed the null-ringdown result from raw with my own estimator and matched all six 29 October preregistration hashes to prefixes of the diary. Today the raw data are not public, five commits are unpushed and the licence is ambiguous. Venue: Zenodo now.

(d) Teaching note. This is the best chance of a peer-reviewed paper. A bench costing a few hundred dollars shows ringdown against linewidth, a receiver null that is not a modal null, and a termination frequency pull that yields the electromechanical coupling. I also found that the transmitter-junction voltage dips 4 to 11% at each resonance. By circuit theory the linewidth of zA/zB therefore leaves out the driven transducer's resistor loss, which predicts a median ringdown-to-linewidth ratio of 0.92; the diary reports 0.91 as an unresolved gap. Window by window the agreement is loose, so this needs a bench check. Venue: American Journal of Physics, European Journal of Physics or Physics Education. Needed first: a second plate, a thermometer, finer frequency steps, a source-side reference tap and a cost table.

(e) New physics. None that I could defend. The null test is local-versus-global control (Elliott et al. 1988) and multi-reference modal testing; the frequency pull is shunted-piezo theory (Hagood and von Flotow 1991); fingerprint and handling sensitivity are resonance inspection and impedance-based monitoring (Park et al. 2003).

The single output that would do the most good is (a) built on (c): one plainly titled paper on what a linear glass plate does and does not compute, whose spine is the artefact catalogue with open data. It is where the project's unique asset meets a documented gap. It needs no novelty claim that prior art can defeat. Most of its evidence already exists. And it corrects the project's own public record: the site source still lists the March papers for download without a notice. The case study is the cheapest second output, and the teaching note is the safest credit.
