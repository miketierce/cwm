### 1. O3 ringdown and ring-up: preregistration (written 13:11, before any data)
**Question:** on this mount, does each mode decay exponentially once the drive stops, at the rate its resonance width predicts (τ = Q/πf₀)? Ring-up, when the drive starts, is measured alongside as a second method and as the fallback if the ringdown proves too hard to catch (operator's suggestion).

**Why before the teardown:**
- The setup is characterized, so a failure points at the capture method rather than at a new rig.
- Frequency and decay time do not depend on gain, so a working ringdown is a readout that ignores the gain drift seen in every FT-1 session (O1).
- O5 (TX pins open vs shorted) would reuse this capture.

**Wiring:**
- **Part 1, TX1 path** (`--path F1`): Ch B on the TX1 junction (moved by the operator, reported today); Ch A on the preamp output.
- **Part 2, TX2 path** (`--path F2`): Ch B back on the TX2 junction, the start wiring.
- Both channels ±5 V, DC; 10% duty. Off state: pins LOW (`Foff`), TX shorted through 220 Ω, as in every FT-1 capture. No firmware change.
- The FT-1 tools are unchanged (capture `05f3c36c…`, scoring `cce97f35…`). The capture script imports `Bench`, `demod` and `pio_actual_freq` from the capture tool and `fit` from `overnight_20261026/fit_zoom.py` (`003f95cc…`) without modifying either.

**Procedure** (`ringdown_20261029/ring_capture.py`, sha256 `c30f1f6dd40db22d…`), per path:
1. **Live check** at the path's check frequency (Ch B must see the drive; |H| in the handling-map range).
2. **QUIET:** 10 captures of 8064 samples (10.3 ms at 781.25 kS/s), drive off.
3. **Zoom sweep:** every distinct NCO frequency within ±400 Hz of the path's six overnight windows, 2 repeats, random order (seed 20261029), FT-1 settings (3072 samples, 40 ms settle). Each window is fitted with `fit_zoom.fit` on complex H = zA/zB. The drive frequency is the NCO command nearest the fitted f₀, or the zoom peak when the fit misses the overnight cuts.
4. **Timing qualification** at 65 kHz, 15 cycles, not scored. It sets the delay between starting a block and sending `Foff`.
5. **Per window,** in random order, cycles of:
   - **ring-up:** the scope is armed on Ch B rising through 1 V (10% pre-trigger), then the drive starts, so the block is aligned to the first drive pulse;
   - **ringdown:** after ≥ 150 ms of drive a free-running block starts and `Foff` follows after the adapted delay. The stop is the last drive pulse on Ch B plus half a period, and the capture is kept if the stop lies 1.0–4.0 ms into the block.
   - Cycles continue until 10 ringdowns are kept or 60 cycles have run.

**Scoring** (`ringdown_20261029/score_ring.py`, sha256 `33155464bea4829a…`):
- **Envelope:** Ch A mixed to baseband at the drive frequency, 0.4 ms Hann low-pass.
- **Ringdown model:** z = D·e^{s(t − t_stop)} from t_stop + 0.3 ms. The fit ends 0.25 ms before the end of the block, or where the fitted envelope falls to the larger of 5× the QUIET envelope noise and 3 ADC steps (117 mV). Below that the 8-bit quantization biases the tail: QUIET noise is only about 0.3 of a step. A fit window shorter than 1 ms is not scored.
- **Ring-up model:** z = C + D·e^{s(t − t₀)} from t₀ + 0.3 ms to 0.25 ms before the end of the block.
- In both, s = −1/τ + 2πi·Δf, by complex least squares. R² compares |z| with the fitted magnitude over the fit window.
- **Captures:** re-classified from raw Ch B with the capture script's own rules; clipped or overflowed captures are dropped.
- **Per window:** medians over at least 5 scored captures.
- **τ_Q = Q/πf₀** from the same run's zoom fit, used only if the fit passes the overnight cuts: f₀ in window, se(f₀) ≤ 20 Hz, relative rms ≤ 0.10, se(ln Q) ≤ 0.15.
- **Self-test** (planted decays through the same code, run before any data): all six planted decays (three ringdown, three ring-up) recovered within 2%. Ringdown: −0.44%, −0.06%, −1.03%; ring-up: −1.33%, +0.12%, −1.79%. A doublet 300 Hz apart is rejected, and the stop classification is right in 4 of 4 cases.

**Expected, from prior data** (Oct 28 sweeps 252 and 254 refitted with this code): 10 of 12 windows give a usable Q.

**Predictions:**
- **P1 (primary, O3's criterion):** at least 5 windows, across both paths, pass on ringdown: median R² ≥ 0.9 over ≥ 5 scored captures, and τ within 20% of τ_Q.
- **P2:** the same holds on ring-up.
- **P3:** where a window passes on both, ring-up and ringdown τ agree within 10%.

**Outcomes:**
- **Confirmed:** at least 5 windows pass. **Partial:** 1–4 pass. **Not confirmed:** none pass, with a valid capture. P2 is scored the same way.
- **Validity:** a run's ringdown capture is valid if at least half its windows have ≥ 5 scored ringdowns and at most 5% of its target captures clip or overflow; the same rule applies to the ring-up.
- **Fallback:** if no run gives a valid ringdown capture, the ringdown is reported as not captured, and the ring-up becomes O3's method of record.
- If only Part 1 runs, the criterion is unchanged.

**Not tested here:** O5 (TX open vs shorted), the temporal-memory question (O6), or anything about computation. A pass validates a gain-independent readout of f₀ and τ on this mount.

**Use:** checks direct ringdown before the teardown; the per-mode τ is the "before" for the rework.

**Preregistered outcomes:**

- **P2 (ring-up): confirmed.** 8 of 10 pass; F1:99500 (0.76) and F1:103000 (1.29) fail.

**Diagnostics (not preregistered):**
- **Same modes:** each ringdown's free frequency (drive plus fitted Δf) is within 0.1 linewidth of the zoom f₀.
- **Precision:** per-capture τ varies by about 1% (interquartile range), so the gaps to τ_Q are systematic, not noise.
- **Ringdowns decay faster than the linewidths predict:** median ringdown τ/τ_Q is 0.91 (0.83–1.16 in the passing windows); for the ring-up it is 0.98. Not resolved. Candidates:
  - a Q bias from the zoom model's background term;
  - a nearby mode inside the passband of the 0.4 ms envelope filter;

- **The two methods disagree where doublets were already suspected:** near 90.5 kHz (the two paths peak 52 Hz apart, Oct 27 §2) and near 97 kHz (F2:97000 misses the zoom cuts, and its ringdown is not exponential, R² 0.70). A single-mode fit sees a second mode differently in a ring-up (both steady states plus both transients) than in a ringdown (transients only).
- F1:103000's zoom Q has the largest uncertainty of the usable windows (11%).

**What it means:**
- **Direct ringdown works on this bench:** every capture was caught, τ comes to about 1% per capture, and the free decay gives f₀, all in a 5 ms block. The May failure (T4.2) came from the shared PZT and from captures that started 6–48 ms after the stop, not from the plate.
- **For sensing,** f₀ and τ come without an amplitude calibration, and amplitude is what drifted between FT-1 sessions.
- **For the temporal question (O6),** these are the plate's memory times on this mount: 1–2 ms between 45 and 117 kHz, that is 2–4 symbols at 2 kHz, as O6 assumed.
- **Open:** the ~9% gap between ringdown and linewidth, and the doublet regions. A two-mode fit, the ±2 V range, or a 50% duty capture would separate the explanations.
- **Next in this family:** O5 (TX pins open vs shorted) can reuse this capture once the firmware has a tri-state option.

### 5. Two-TX null with ringdown: preregistration (written 14:16, before any data)
Exploratory, operator-approved before the teardown. Commit `3d3b4ac` holds §1–4.

**Question:** when TX1 and TX2 are tuned to cancel at the receiver, is the mode left unexcited (a modal null: the plate is quiet once the drive stops), or does the plate hold energy the receiver could not see (it rings after the stop)?
- This is the physical question behind the E9-style "electronic erase" (June 3), which had no electrical-only control.
- After the stop the receiver sees only the plate: no drive and no feedthrough.

**Wiring:**
- As in §2 Part 2: Ch B on the TX2 junction, Ch A on the preamp output.
- Ch A runs at ±2 V, not ±5 V. A residual of a few mV is then dithered by the noise rather than lost below one ADC step. Ch B stays at ±5 V.
- Off state: pins LOW. No firmware change: `A1`/`A2` and `PH1`/`PH2` already exist, and both channels sit in phase-locked Block A.

**Targets** (O3's drive commands):
- **58,510 → 58,523.0 Hz,** O3's cleanest single mode (ringdown R² 0.996, τ 0.94 ms). Both TXs drive it (Oct 28 grid: TX1 857 mV, TX2 392 mV).
- **90,615 → 90,647.5 Hz,** the suspected doublet. TX1's zoom peak (90,651 Hz; its fit misses the cuts) and TX2's (90,591 Hz) are 60 Hz apart, and both TXs drive it hard (792 and 858 mV).

**Procedure** (`nullring_20261029/null_capture.py`, sha256 `e0ae45639a40addd…`):
1. **Checks:** live check (TX2 at 60 kHz), QUIET ×10, then a TX2 qualification at 65 kHz (10 cycles, not scored).
2. **Null calibration, per target:**
   - H = zA/zB against the TX2 drive. TX1's duty and phase are solved, then refined up to 5 times, aiming for ρ ≤ 0.015.
   - TX2 runs at 100‰, or 200‰ if TX1 would otherwise need less than 70‰.
3. **Ringdowns, per target:** 10 cycles of the three conditions, in random order within each cycle:
   - **S1:** TX1 alone at the null setting;
   - **S2:** TX2 alone;
   - **N:** both together.
   - Each cycle: `Foff`, 120 ms of silence, start, 150 ms of drive, then a free-running block, with `Foff` timed to land the stop 1.25 ms in.
   - S1 has no TX2 pulses, so its stop is placed from its delay plus the scope offset (O3: 7.80 ms, p10–p90 within ±0.05 ms).

**Scoring** (`nullring_20261029/score_null.py`, sha256 `749b4a3670598c9b…`):
- **ρ_drive** = |mean H_N| / |mean H_S2|, from the steady state before each stop (Hann DFT from the block start to 0.05 ms before the stop).
- **ρ_free** = rms|Z_N| / rms|Z_S2| over 0.3–1.5 ms after the stop. Z is the coherent mean of the TX2-referenced envelopes (0.4 ms Hann, as in O3).
- **Verdict:** a **modal null** if ρ_free ≤ max(2ρ_drive, 0.04); **still rings** if ρ_free ≥ max(3ρ_drive, 0.08); indeterminate in between.
- **Superposition:** rms(|Z_N − Z_S2| − |Z_S1|) / rms|Z_S1| ≤ 0.10.
- **Valid** if every condition keeps ≥ 5 captures and ρ_drive ≤ 0.05.
- **Self-test** (planted data, before any bench data):
  - A single mode with a 1.5% drive null reads as a modal null (ρ_free 0.019).
  - A 60 Hz doublet with the same drive null reads as still ringing (ρ_free 0.195). Its N/S2 ratio rises from 0.08 to 0.50 over 2 ms as the two modes drift out of phase.
  - Superposition error: 0.003 and 0.001.
  - The null-tuning loop converges in 1–2 steps on simulated plates, including one whose duty model is 8% off.

**Predictions:**
- **Q1, 58.5 kHz: modal null.** With a single mode, the receiver sees all of it, so cancelling it at the receiver cancels the mode.
- **Q2, 90.6 kHz: still rings.** If two modes about 60 Hz apart share this window, a receiver null leaves both excited. After the stop they drift out of phase, so the residual grows before it decays.
- **Q3:** superposition holds within 10% at both frequencies.
- Each question is scored confirmed, not confirmed (the opposite verdict), indeterminate, or invalid.

**What it shows and doesn't:** what an interference "erase" leaves in the plate, tested by linear superposition. It is not a computing claim; with the mode structure known, every outcome follows from single-TX measurements. Q2 also tests whether the 90.6 kHz window holds two modes, as O3 suspected.

### 7. Null result
`score_null.py` → `null_result.json`. The diary and script hashes matched the §5 record before scoring.

| Target | Drive null ρ_drive | After the stop ρ_free | N/S2 at 0.3 / 1.0 / 2.0 ms | Verdict | Predicted | Superposition |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 58.5 kHz | 0.0066 (−44 dB) | 0.47 | 0.20 / 0.54 / 0.77 | still rings | modal null ✗ | 0.014 ✓ |
| 90.6 kHz | 0.0005 (−66 dB) | 0.25 | 0.08 / 0.30 / 0.43 | still rings | still rings ✓ | 0.020 ✓ |

The noise floor is about 0.0004 in ρ at both frequencies. Both targets are valid.

**Preregistered outcomes:**
- **Q1, 58.5 kHz modal null: not confirmed.** The receiver null was −44 dB while driving, yet after the stop the plate rang at 47% of TX2-alone's level, and the residual kept growing.
- **Q2, 90.6 kHz still rings: confirmed.**
- **Q3, superposition: confirmed at both,** 1.4% and 2.0%.

**Diagnostics (post hoc):**
- **Averaging:** fully coherent, |mean z| / mean|z| = 1.000 for S2 and N at both targets.
- **Each TX's own free decay,** fitted on the averaged envelopes (TX1 = N − S2):

| Target | TX1 alone | TX2 alone |
|---|---|---|
| 58.5 kHz | 58,540 Hz, τ 0.90 ms (R² 0.996) | 58,514 Hz, τ 2.21 ms (R² 0.950) |
| 90.6 kHz | 90,653 Hz, τ 1.60 ms (R² 0.942) | 90,609 Hz, τ 1.67 ms (R² 0.991) |

- **At 58.5 kHz the two TXs excite components that decay 2.5× apart at almost the same frequency.** Cancelling two decays of 0.90 and 2.21 ms leaves a residual of 1 − e^{−t/1.52 ms} relative to S2: 0.18, 0.48 and 0.73 at 0.3, 1.0 and 2.0 ms, against the measured 0.20, 0.54 and 0.77. TX1's component matches O3's TX1 ringdown (0.94 ms); TX2 also reaches a slower one. This is most likely two overlapping modes.
- **At 90.6 kHz the doublet is two modes 44 Hz apart with nearly equal τ,** consistent with O3's zoom peaks (TX1 90,651 Hz, TX2 90,591 Hz).

**What it means:**
- **A receiver null is not a modal null on this plate,** even at a mode that looked clean from one TX. Each TX drives its own mix of overlapping modes, as near-degenerate pairs on a square plate would (sidebar S9). The null cancels their sum at one frequency and one point, and the energy reappears once the drive stops.
- **For an E9-style "electronic erase":** cancelling at the receiver changes what the receiver sees, not what the plate holds. This supports the reading that the rewritable layer sits in the drive settings and the readout, not in the glass.
- **For two-TX designs (FT-3P, template matching):** a null is specific to one receiver position and frequency, and overlapping pairs should be expected. "Single mode" in O3 means single as seen from one TX.
- The failed prediction (Q1) is the more informative result.

**Bench state:** NCO off, duty 500 and phase 0 on CH1 and CH2. Ch B on the TX2 junction (the start wiring). Plate D is ready for the teardown.
