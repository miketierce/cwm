**Small preliminary campaign before October's evidence freeze.** Its purpose is to establish a credible starting point; it should not attempt to finish the entire Phase I project.

1. Recover the readout safely. The [June 30 diary](lab_diary_20260630.md) records RX-board overheating and disconnection. Confirm present condition and repair before power; save the actual restored topology and supply checks.
2. Choose a small set of physical configurations and an appropriate plain-reference or conventional-sensor baseline. Fix acquisition time and measurement budget. Record why this task represents the prospective user's problem.
3. Randomize acquisition order. Include bare repeats, touch/remount controls, drive-off controls, and an appropriate electrical/acoustic-path null. Log temperature, gains, device, operator, reference, and timestamps.
4. Train once, then collect new data on at least three days if recovery allows. Freeze frequency selection, calibration allowance, normalization, and decoder before final evaluation. Report exact or balanced accuracy, confusion matrix, unknown-state behavior, and uncertainty grouped by independent experimental units.
5. Test fewer measurements honestly. If normalization needs extra channels, retain and charge for them. If channels are physically unavailable, do not use their earlier full-capture values to normalize the query.
6. Produce one compact evidence table with the baseline, CWM, and ablations at matched conditions. A negative result must change the proposed mechanism, claim, or risk plan; it should not be hidden by a different headline metric.

**Proposed funded Phase I aims.** Quantitative thresholds below are starting points for preregistration and user discussion, not NSF requirements or achieved performance. Select final thresholds before evaluation and justify them with the intended application.

| Aim | Research question | Deliverable and decision rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1: Mechanism and nuisance separation | Can controlled geometry improve sensitivity to the selected state relative to handling, temperature, and electrical coupling? | Matched geometry/reference dataset and a repeatable target effect surviving relevant nulls; redirect if nuisance or pickup explains the separation |
| 2: Stable calibration and transfer | Can the state decision survive independent days/rewrites with an affordable calibration procedure? | Frozen evaluation protocol; an initial candidate gate is 95% balanced accuracy for the agreed small known-state task, plus separately defined unknown rejection and uncertainty; quantify any recalibration burden |
| 3: Resource advantage and reproducible instrument | Can the useful result be preserved with fewer measurements or lower total acquisition burden? | Candidate gate: a twofold reduction in an agreed resource at comparable error, counting the reference and all processing; another operator reproduces the workflow |

Failure to meet a proposed gate should produce a mechanism explanation and a documented redirect/stop decision. A Phase I grant can fund these uncertainties. A proposal still needs enough preliminary evidence and methodological detail to make the investigation credible.
