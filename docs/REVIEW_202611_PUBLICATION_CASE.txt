===== steelman:science-and-publication =====
BEST CASE:
The strongest honest case is not that the plate does anything new. It is that the project now holds three things other people can use: controlled negative results on a
class of hardware about which positive claims are still being published; a catalogue, with data, of the specific artefacts that made a linear plate look like a memory and
a computer; and a complete record of how LLM-assisted research went wrong and was pulled back.

(a) Negative-results and fair-baseline paper. As physics it is not new: theory says a linear reservoir with a linear readout creates no new features (Bollt 2021; Nokkala
et al. 2026), and Wright et al. (2022) reported their plate as close to a linear classifier. As evidence it is useful. First-round reviewers found no no-substrate control
in six MEMS reservoir papers, and I found a March 2026 preprint reporting a piezoelectric-ceramic reservoir 2.4 points above logistic regression on MNIST (abstract only).
This project ran the missing controls in every October session (a no-plate loopback and a dummy receiver), and its own stored analysis shows a 32-dimension digital random
projection scoring 87.7% against 88.0% for the 240-feature plate, and 99.5% at 240 dimensions. Venue: a journal that already carries reservoir-benchmark critique
(International Journal of Parallel, Emergent and Distributed Systems) or an unconventional-computing journal; arXiv cs.ET or physics.app-ph if an endorser is found,
otherwise Zenodo. Needed first: one preregistered series on the reworked fixture with third-party timestamps, a second plate, a plate thermometer, the temporal-memory
test that was planned and never run (O6), baselines scripted with fixed seeds, and each artefact shown switched on and off.

(b) LLM methods case study. Partly new. Neighbours exist for machine-learning agents (Trehan and Chopra 2026; Beel et al. 2025) and for audit discipline in AI-assisted
writing (Zhou and Yu 2026); in two searches I found no bench-hardware case. The material is unusually complete. I confirmed that v10 (5 March, 11:38) set predictions with
kill criteria; that v11, committed 42 minutes later, already had a "Prediction | Measured" table; that a 6 March commit "corrected" the "ring-down" from 169 to 180 ms
when a wave-speed constant changed; and that the first real rods (7 April) gave Q of 10 to 13 against v10's own kill line of 100. Q then went through 7,687 to 60,092
(April, fits with R-squared under 0.18), 2,275 (May, R-squared 0.077) and finally 136 to 635 (October, two methods agreeing). Venue: a metascience or data-science
journal, or a long essay with a DOI. Needed first: the owner's own prose, an error table with counts and time-to-detection, and legal advice if a non-provisional is still
contemplated, because the essay would state that the specification's measurements were not made.

(c) Instrument and dataset. Low novelty (an open resonant-ultrasound toolbox exists, and impedance-based structural monitoring uses the same glued-disc arrangement), but
it is the foundation for everything else and nearly free. The October raw data (1.1 GB) and scripts reproduce: I recomputed the null-ringdown result from raw with my own
estimator and matched all six 29 October preregistration hashes to prefixes of the diary. Today the raw data are not public, five commits are unpushed and the licence is
ambiguous. Venue: Zenodo now.

(d) Teaching note. This is the best chance of a peer-reviewed paper. A bench costing a few hundred dollars (my estimate) shows ringdown against linewidth, a receiver null
that is not a modal null, and a termination frequency pull that yields the electromechanical coupling. I also found that the transmitter-junction voltage dips 4 to 11% at
each resonance. By circuit theory the linewidth of zA/zB therefore leaves out the driven transducer's resistor loss, which predicts a median ringdown-to-linewidth ratio
of 0.92; the diary reports 0.91 as an unresolved gap. Window by window the agreement is loose, so this needs a bench check. Venue: American Journal of Physics, European
Journal of Physics or Physics Education. Needed first: a second plate, a thermometer, finer frequency steps, a source-side reference tap and a cost table.

(e) New physics. None that I could defend. The null test is local-versus-global control (Elliott et al. 1988) and multi-reference modal testing; the frequency pull is
shunted-piezo theory (Hagood and von Flotow 1991); fingerprint and handling sensitivity are resonance inspection and impedance-based monitoring (Park et al. 2003).

The single output that would do the most good is (a) built on (c): one plainly titled paper on what a linear glass plate does and does not compute, whose spine is the
artefact catalogue with open data. It is where the project's unique asset meets a documented gap. It needs no novelty claim that prior art can defeat. Most of its
evidence already exists. And it corrects the project's own public record: the site source still lists the March papers for download, labelled only as prior versions, with
no statement that their measurements were not made. The case study is the cheapest second output, and the teaching note is the safest credit.

HONEST ODDS: 
New physics: under 2%. I looked for an anomaly in the October data and found a conventional explanation instead.

Deposit: if attempted, about 90% that it is complete and usable; about 25 to 35% that anyone outside reuses it within two years.

Baseline and pitfalls paper: about 50% that one person brings it to a submission-ready state within six months. Given that, about 45% that it appears on arXiv
(endorsement roughly 55%, moderation roughly 80%) and about 50% that it finds a peer-reviewed home within 18 months at a specialist or soundness-reviewed venue; about 10%
at a selective physics journal. Unconditionally, roughly one chance in four of a peer-reviewed paper.

Teaching note: about 50% acceptance at one of three education journals if written to standard after the rebuild; about 30% unconditionally.

Case study: about 30% for a peer-reviewed home; about 15% that an essay version is widely read.

Impact: about 10% that the best output gathers ten citations in three years; 3 to 5% that it visibly changes practice. The realistic good outcome is a small number of
careful readers who reuse the controls.

About 40% that everything stops at a deposit and a blog post because endorsement or expert feedback is negative. About 10 to 15% that publishing causes real cost to the
owner (association with AI-written work; friction from stating in public that the specification's measurements were not made), lower with candid framing and legal advice.

Main uncertainty in these numbers: my literature check was thin. Three web searches and about twenty-five fetches, abstracts and Crossref records only, no full texts. The
claim that no bench-hardware case study exists rests on two searches.

WHERE CONSENSUS IS RIGHT:
I tried to challenge these and could not.

1. The March papers contain no hardware measurements. I confirmed it in primary files: v18 labels 98.5 dB as 'Measured' and gives a ring-down of 180 ms; commit 5e0d985
changed the 'ring-down' from 169 to 180 ms along with the wave speed; v11's 'Measured' column was committed 42 minutes after v10's predictions; the first rods on 7 April
gave Q of about 10 to 13.

2. v19r should not be posted. Its own ledger still lists 4,096 states, 80/80 and the CHSH figure as measured, and its Q of 2,275 comes from a fit with R-squared 0.077.

3. No October result is new physics. The null test is what a plate with modal overlap near one must do, and matches local-control and multi-reference modal-testing
practice. The frequency pull is textbook shunted-piezo behaviour. Ringdown agreeing with linewidth is an identity for a linear resonator. My junction analysis strengthens
this: the one 'unexplained' gap has an ordinary explanation.

4. Fair digital baselines match or beat the plate. The project's own stored file shows a 32-dimension random projection equal to the plate and a 240-dimension one eleven
points ahead. I could not verify the handoff's later 'CAM recheck'; its only record is said to be a session transcript outside the repositories, which I was not permitted
to read.

5. Prior art is dense. I found more of it, not less.

6. arXiv is a real gate. An unaffiliated first-time author needs a personal endorser, AI use must be declared, and the text must be the author's own. The diaries and
scripts were written by an LLM under the owner's direction (handoff section 1), which any submission must say.

7. The preregistration dates are self-attested, the fixture does not hold the transfer function between sessions (3 to 10% change against 0.5% within), there is one
plate, and no plate thermometer. None of the outputs I argue for can skip the rebuild.

8. The benefit is not in the physics. Where I differ from the consensus is only on how much the controls, the catalogue and the corrected record are worth to others, and
on the status of three specific statements (rank near 1, the unexplained 9%, the unmeasured memory test).
