# Lab Diary — October 21, 2026

## Bench recovery after the June 30 preamp failure: root cause found, preamp working, acoustic path verified

First powered session since June 30. Goal: find why the RX preamp keeps overheating, restore a working readout, and get the bench to a state where the PR #16 fast track (FT-0…FT-6, merged to main as `32f4a94`) can start.

### Starting state
- Last record (June 30): RX/preamp board overheated, disconnected, **do not re-power until cold-inspected**. No bench work between July and today.
- Pico NCO and PicoScope 2204A were still on USB and both responded before any powered work: NCO firmware v2.0 (126 MHz clock, all 15 channels idle, die 17.2 °C) on `/dev/cu.usbmodem113401`; scope opened via `ps2000` as `2204A`.
- Bench as found:
  - One 25×76 mm microscope slide (old putty pattern on it) carrying three PZTs:
    - **W-centre TX**, driven through Board D (powered from the NCO)
    - **SE TX**, driven directly from the NCO
    - **NE RX**, wired to preamp pin 3 through R + C
  - A loose **dummy PZT** wired so it can be swapped in for the live RX (ELECTRICAL_ONLY control).
  - **Ch B** sits on Board D's 4.7 kΩ "TX bundle" tap, which sums every NCO output. That is why it appeared to hear every TX.

### Second overheat event
The preamp batteries were connected before the cold inspection. **The chip overheated again, and the chip was the first part to get hot.** That pointed at the supply wiring rather than a passive part.

### Root cause: battery leads swapped on the bottom rails
- Six board photos were taken for remote inspection. They were not kept, since they show the old mis-wired board.
- My first read of the photos was **wrong and is retracted**. It claimed pin 8 was grounded and pin 7 was on +9V. Checked at the bench, the chip-side wiring matches `companion/readout_preamp_wiring.html`:
  - pin 8 → +9V
  - pins 7–6 jumpered
  - pin 5 → ground
  - pin 4 → −9V
  - orange jumper ties the top blue rail to the bottom red rail (ground)
- The actual fault was in the battery leads:

| Rail | Was wired to | Should be |
|---|---|---|
| Top red (+9V) | Battery A red | Battery A red ✓ |
| Bottom blue (−9V) | **A-black + B-red twist** (the series midpoint) | Battery B black |
| Bottom red (ground) | **Battery B black** | The A-black + B-red twist |

- Effect, measured from board ground:
  - V− (pin 4) sat at **+9V** and V+ (pin 8) at **+18V**.
  - Pin 5, tied to ground, therefore sat 9V *below* V−. The input protection diode conducted battery current straight through the die. This explains "chip hot first".
  - The −9V-side 10 µF electrolytic was reverse-biased at the same time.
- The same wiring was almost certainly the June 30 cause too: the build was never changed.
- **Fix:** swap the two bottom-rail leads.
- **Meter checks (chip out):**
  - No shorts between any pair of rails.
  - Rails after the swap: **+8V / −6V**.

### Batteries
- Four 9V cells measured: two under 2V (discarded), one about 8V, one about 6.5V.
- The board is running on uneven +8/−6V rails. That is fine for the OPA2134 (works down to ±2.5V) but it limits output swing to about 6V on the negative side.

### Rebuild
- Replaced the −9V-side 10 µF.
  - It first went in with **+ on pin 4 (wrong)**.
  - Corrected to: + leg on ground, − leg on pin 4.
  - Rule: + always goes on the more positive node.
- Rewired and added decoupling capacitors.
- Inserted a spare OPA2134. On power-up it **stayed at room temperature**.

### Output stuck at the +rail: missing input bias resistor
- Ch A idled at **+7.44V DC**. My diagnostic stopped itself before driving any tones.
- Moving the BNC (red → pin 1, black → ground) changed nothing.
- Meter readings: pin 1 **+7.5V**, pin 3 **0.17V**, pin 2 **0.15V**, pin 7 **0V**.
  - Pin 2 equals pin 1 ÷ 48, so the 47 kΩ / 1 kΩ feedback network is intact.
  - Pin 7 at 0V means the spare half works.
  - Pin 3 at 0.17V asks the ×48 DC-coupled stage for about 8.2V, more than the rail allows.
  - **The chip was healthy.**
- **The 100 kΩ from pin 3 to ground was missing.** Without it, the FET input floats and drags the output to a rail.
- With it added, the output idles at **about +1.0–1.08V DC** (about 21 mV referred to the input).
  - This is still unexplained: it is above this chip's typical offset of under 1 mV, and it stays the same with the RX unplugged.
  - Not yet fixed.
- Side note: a meter "OL" reading during this step came from a wrong meter mode, not from the circuit.

### Channel-independent pickup on the RX path
All levels below are at the preamp output, scope ±10V range.

**Slide RX:**
- Every NCO channel produced the same Ch A signal, including channels not wired to the slide:
  - CH1 / CH15 at 50 kHz: about 2.1–2.3 Vpp
  - at 120 kHz: 0.45–0.63 Vpp
  - at 250 kHz: 0.7–0.8 Vpp
- In the full sweep, the response curves of four channels matched within about 2%. They stayed strong above the stage's ~166 kHz bandwidth and overloaded the ±1V range.
- **Electrical, not acoustic.**
- The "peaks" in that sweep (57, 61, 63.5 kHz…) are **not slide modes**.
- Stage 1 did confirm that **all 15 NCO channels output** (seen on Ch B).

**Isolation steps:**

| Step | Result |
|---|---|
| Added a shared ground between the preamp and NCO boards | No change. Kept anyway: it is correct practice. |
| Unplugged the RX lead from pin 3 | Ch A dropped to the floor on every channel. The pickup enters via the RX wiring. |
| Reseated loose NCO connections | Slide TX leads found at NCO breadboard f–j rows 15 and 17 |

- Rows 15 and 17 → GP20/**CH11** and GP18/**CH10**.
  - This mapping assumes USB toward row 1 with the Pico pins in columns c/h.
  - **Not verified by measurement.**

**RX moved to a 100×100 mm plate:**
- The pickup changed shape but stayed the same on every channel: 0.36–0.45 Vpp at 50 kHz, **1.43 Vpp at 120 kHz**, 0.36 Vpp at 250 kHz.
- A 15-channel sweep (30–120 kHz, 250 Hz steps), with each channel normalised to the cross-channel median, found no channel-specific response (largest ratio 1.4×).
  - The pickup was as large as the real signal, so this method could not see the plate.
  - That sweep's JSON was overwritten by a later run; only this summary remains.

**Fix:** unplugged every NCO lead except this plate's two TX.
- Their NCO breadboard positions are f–j **rows 12 and 20** → GP22/**CH12** and GP16/**CH9**, both Block C (PWM).
- **Confirmed by measurement:**
  - CH1, now connected to nothing, gives under 0.3% of full scale.
  - CH9 and CH12 give strong responses.
- **The common pickup came from the unused drive leads.**

### Acoustic attribution: live vs null RX (100×100 plate, CH9/CH12, 30–120 kHz)
Values are the drive-frequency tone at the preamp output, scope ±10V range, 361 points, 250 Hz steps.

| | Live RX median | Null RX median / max | Live / null (median) |
|---|---|---|---|
| CH9 | 2.16V | 1 / 6 mV | **≥1,587×** |
| CH12 | 2.55V | 1 / 5 mV | **≥1,646×** |

- The ratio holds in every 15 kHz band on both channels.
- The null readings are at this range's noise floor, so the true margin may be larger.
- Consistent with the historical "PZT-lifted null = 0% feedthrough" result.
- The live response varies about 170× across frequency, with dense peaks and notches: modal structure, not a smooth leakage curve.

### Clipping and drive level
- **At 50% duty (full drive), peaks clip:**
  - Every band tops out at about 6.0–6.3V.
  - 43 of 361 points (CH9) and 31 of 361 (CH12) exceed 5.5V.
- **At 10% duty (`A9:100`, `A12:100`), nothing clips:**
  - Highest peak 4.84V (CH9) and 4.54V (CH12); no point above 5.5V.
  - Signal level fell to 0.32× (CH9) and 0.31× (CH12) of full drive.
  - Predicted: sin(π·0.1) = 0.309.
  - **The chain is linear in drive amplitude.**
  - Duty was restored to 500 afterwards.
- Highest peaks at 10% duty (kHz):
  - **CH9:** 35.75, 38.75, 42.00, 54.25, 63.25, **73.75**, 78.75, 90.50, 105.00, 106.00
  - **CH12:** 34.50, 36.25, 41.25, 54.25, 58.50, 86.75, 90.25, 90.75, **97.00**, 110.75
  - 54.25 kHz and about 90.3–90.5 kHz appear on both TX, as expected for one plate.

### Follow-up: offset fix, TX moved to Block A, Ch B as a single-source reference
**Changes:**
- **Offset fix:** the 1 kΩ's ground leg now goes through a 10 µF to ground (+ on the resistor side). The stage is ×1 at DC and ×48 above about 16 Hz.
- **TX moved to Block A:** both plate TX leads now come from NCO a–e **rows 4/5** (GP2/GP3 = **CH1/CH2**), so `PH2` can set their relative phase.
- **Ch B moved off the bundle tap:**
  - Ch B came off Board D's bundle tap.
  - The row-5 220 Ω now runs to its own junction row, which holds the TX2 PZT lead and Ch B's signal pin.
  - **Ch B = CH2 drive at the PZT terminal, after its 220 Ω.**
- TX1's 220 Ω is still crimped to its lead, with no junction row yet.

**Check:** idle offset, then CH1/CH2/CH9/CH12 plus CH3 (nothing connected) driven one at a time at 10% duty. Sweep 50–110 kHz in 1 kHz steps. Ch A on the ±10V range, Ch B on ±5V.

| | Ch A median / max | Ch B median / max |
|---|---|---|
| Idle DC | **−64 mV** (±1V range; was about +1.0V) | |
| CH1 (row 4) | 852 mV / 3.31V | 9 mV / 47 mV |
| CH2 (row 5) | 809 mV / 3.26V | **545 mV / 600 mV** |
| CH9, CH12 (old rows) | about 1 mV (floor) | about 1 mV |
| CH3 (control) | about 1 mV (floor) | about 1 mV |

**Findings:**
- **The offset is fixed.** Peaks at 10% duty now reach 3.3V and nothing clips.
- **Rows 4/5 = CH1/CH2 is confirmed.** That validates the assumed Pico placement, and with it the earlier row-to-channel mappings.
- **Ch B sees CH2 only.**
  - Its 545–600 mV is about 15% below the unloaded fundamental, (2·3.3/π)·sin(0.1π) ≈ 0.65V.
  - The shortfall changes with frequency. That is PZT loading of the 220 Ω, which this tap point is meant to capture.
- **CH1 leaks into Ch B:**
  - About 1.7% at the median, up to about 8% at worst. Probably capacitive pickup between neighbouring breadboard rows.
  - No effect on single-TX runs.
  - In FT-3P it could skew the reference phase by about 1° typically, up to ~5° at worst. Record it during the phase qualification; try moving the junction row further from row 4.
- Data: `chb_tap_and_tx_map.json`.

- PZT layout:

| PZT | Position | Connection |
|---|---|---|
| RX | top centre | preamp pin 3 → Ch A |

| TX2 | SW corner | NCO **CH2** (a–e row 5); **Ch B reference** |
| Spare | NE and NW corners | present, unconnected; keep that way every session |

**Tool:** `tools/k_eff_ft1_capture.py`, tests in `tests/test_k_eff_ft1_capture.py` (8 passing).
- **Session layout:** one invocation per path-state block (QUIET, LOOPBACK, GLASS, ELECTRICAL_ONLY), then `--summarize`.
- **Per trial:**
  - one raw two-channel acquisition at one frequency and repeat, in randomized order;
  - the tool waits for the NCO's `DDS…` acknowledgement, then a 40 ms settle;
  - complex coefficients for both channels by a Hann-weighted single-frequency DFT at the quantized NCO frequency, SYS_CLK / round(SYS_CLK/f);
  - overflow and clip flags.
- **Storage:**
  - Raw int16 goes to `data/raw/k_eff/<session>/` (gitignored), with SHA-256 hashes in the manifest.
  - The trial table, manifest and derived summary go to `data/results/k_eff/<session>/`.
- **The manifest records:**
  - scope model, serial and calibration date;
  - actual sample interval;
  - NCO status;
  - RP2040 die temperature at start, at end and every 200 trials (labeled as die, not resonator, temperature);
  - git commit, dirty flag and script hash;
  - per-block seed;
  - thresholds frozen in advance;
  - any unavailable quantities, with reasons.
- **Grid freeze:** the grid is frozen in `data/results/k_eff/ft1_frozen_grid.json` on the first real block. The tool refuses any later block or session on a different grid.

### Lessons
1. **The wiring doc names the rails but never says which battery lead goes where.** Its ground rails include a red-striped one, which invites exactly this swap. Before inserting a chip, measure the rails with the socket empty.
2. **A floating op-amp input looks like a dead chip.** Measure pins 1, 2, 3 and 7 before replacing parts. Pin 2 = pin 1 ÷ gain shows the feedback network is intact.
3. **Unused NCO leads still plugged in injected channel-independent pickup** about as large as the real signal. Unplug drive leads that aren't in use.
4. **Use a channel with nothing connected as the control.** Cross-channel normalisation fails once pickup is as large as the signal.
5. **Photo-based wiring traces at an angle are unreliable.** Confirm with a meter or by eye at the bench before acting on one.
6. **Keep the dummy-RX swap.** It gave the cleanest attribution in this session.
