### 1. Before S3: cold start and air-temperature readings (10:09)
**Cold start:** the operator unplugged the NCO and disconnected the preamp batteries by 10:09 (reported then). S3 starts no earlier than about 11:10.

**How the air temperature has been read (operator, 10:09):** an instant-read probe thermometer, held by hand in the air space around the glass. This applies to every manual reading so far: the start and end of S1 and S2, and 19:30 and 22:14 during the overnight run.

**What this means for the evening steps (interpretation):**
- Each reading put a hand and the probe into the space around the plate, close to the foam and to the jumpers that hang in front of it. Only friction holds the plate (Oct 26 §7).
- Both readings during the overnight run were followed by a step (8.5% and 3.4% median complex change on CH2). The 22:35–22:37 visit (photo, rails at the breadboard) was not, and nothing changed in 8 hours with nobody at the bench (Oct 26 §8).
- **The steps persisted.** Against cycle 10, cycles 11, 12, 13 and 15 differ by 8.5, 8.7, 8.5 and 7.4%. Against cycle 19, cycles 20, 21 and 22 differ by 3.4, 3.9 and 4.1%.
- **Body heat does not explain them.** A hand or body close to the glass for under a minute warms it by roughly a tenth of a degree (a few tenths at most), and that fades within about half an hour. Overnight, a die cooling of 0.9–1.4°C over hours moved CH2 by 4.4–4.8%. A brief warming of the glass is therefore worth about 1% at most, and it would not last an hour.
- **Electrical coupling from the body does not explain them.** It acts only while the plate is driven, and the NCO was off during both readings (cycle 10 ended 19:13:21, cycle 19 at 22:13:16; cycles 9 → 10 and 18 → 19 were undisturbed at 0.9% and 0.7%).
- So the steps are mechanical: the plate or its leads moved and stayed moved. The most likely trigger is contact during the reach (the probe, a hand or a sleeve brushing the foam or a jumper) or pressure on the bench.
- The 14:22 reading at the end of S2 falls in the window of the post-S2 change (Oct 26 §5), alongside the TX2 lead move. Either could have caused it, and the data cannot separate them. The S1 → S2 change likewise spans two readings (S1 end, S2 start) as well as the power cycle.
- S1 and S2 each recorded one air temperature for all seven blocks (22.7°C and 22.9°C in the manifests), and their logs show readings only at the start and end. No reading fell inside either session.

**For S3 (procedure only):**
- The air temperature is read at arm's length from the plate, at about its height, without leaning on the bench. It is not read in the space around the glass.
- One reading before the first block and one after the last. The probe is held still until the reading stops changing (about a minute in still air).
- `--ambient-temp-c` carries the start reading for every block, as in S1 and S2.
- S3's reading is therefore room air near the bench, not air next to the plate. Only the exploratory drift comparison (O2) uses it. The preregistered tiers do not.
- Nothing else changes: blocks, flags, thresholds and analysis stay as preregistered.

### 2. Overnight zoom sweeps: mode frequency and Q (O4, exploratory, 10:15–10:35)
Fitted during the cold-start wait. Nothing here enters the FT-1 analysis.

**Method:** `overnight_20261026/fit_zoom.py` → `zoom_fits.json`.
- Every cycle swept each distinct NCO frequency within ±400 Hz of the 6 strongest S2 peaks per path, once each: 8–51 points per window at the NCO's 16–108 Hz steps.
- Per window and cycle, one resonance plus background: complex on CH2 (zA/zB, with a linear background term when the window has ≥ 10 points), magnitude only on CH1. CH1's magnitude fixes f₀ and Q but not the peak height.
- Shifts are in ppm and in linewidths (f₀/Q). Each window's yardstick is its median change between consecutive undisturbed cycles (21–45).

**Fit quality:**
- 10 of 12 windows give a usable f₀, and 8 a usable Q. Linewidths are 124–308 Hz (Q 146–732).
- Excluded: CH2 97.0 kHz (residual 19% of the peak) and CH1 103.0 kHz (f₀ se 42 Hz). The one-mode model does not fit; each window probably holds two modes. CH1 90.5 and 97.0 kHz give f₀ but not Q (Q se 24–31%).
- Absolute f₀ is uncertain by 1–16 Hz because the lineshape fit is imperfect. The misfit repeats from cycle to cycle, so changes are much sharper: 5–13 ppm between undisturbed cycles.
- Near 90.5 kHz the two paths peak 52 Hz apart (90,575 and 90,627 Hz), so different modes dominate them. This is a doublet candidate (O4, O7).

**The bench events, in mode terms** (median over the usable windows):

| Event | Cycles | Mode shift | In linewidths | Q change |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Undisturbed, per 20 min | 21–45 | 5–13 ppm | ≤ 0.005 | 0.1–0.5% |
| 16:41 TX2 lead restore | 2 → 3 | 10 ppm | 0.004 | 0.8% |
| 17:53–18:13 (60 kHz step) | 6 → 7 | 10 ppm | 0.003 | 0.3% |
| 19:32 step | 10 → 11 | 82 ppm | 0.046 | 2.6% |
| 22:32 step | 19 → 20 | 41 ppm | 0.018 | 1.6% |

- **The two steps moved the modes by different amounts and in different directions.**
  - 19:32: the CH1 45 kHz mode moved +957 ppm (43 Hz, 0.16 linewidths) and lost 15% of its Q; 58.5 kHz moved +336 ppm. The CH2 modes moved −51 to +46 ppm, with Q changes of −3.5 to +8.1% and peak-height changes of −7.4 to +10.1%.
  - 22:32: the CH2 modes all moved up, +31 to +70 ppm. CH1 45 kHz moved +414 ppm and 58.5 kHz −40 ppm.
  - This fits a change in how the plate touches its supports. A change in the glass as a whole would move every mode by the same fraction.
- The lead restore and the 60 kHz step barely moved the fitted modes. The 60 kHz step lies outside every zoom window.

**The undisturbed night:**
- Most modes followed temperature, upward with warming: 8 of 10 moved +43 to +89 ppm per °C of Pico die temperature (r = 0.81–0.87). Fused silica stiffens as it warms, so this is the expected sign.
- Two modes did not, which also rules out the NCO clock (a clock error moves every mode equally):
  - CH2 73.5 kHz did not move with temperature (−7 ± 6 ppm/°C);
  - CH1 45 kHz moved the opposite way (−131 ± 23 ppm/°C).
  - These are the two modes the steps changed most (73.5 kHz Q +8.1%, 45 kHz +957 ppm), so the mount probably shapes their response.
- A common shift across the 10 modes gives +43 ± 6 ppm/°C, down to −76 ppm at 04:32. It accounts for 47% of the night's frequency variance; the rest is mode-specific, mostly the 45 kHz mode.
- Largest excursion from cycle 21: 22–246 ppm, which is 0.008–0.069 linewidths.
- Q fell as the room cooled. CH2 slopes are +1.3 to +3.8% per °C except at 106 kHz; 73.5 kHz reached −5.8%.
- The die temperature is a proxy for the room, read in 0.47°C steps. The slopes stay provisional until a thermometer sits on the plate (O2).

**Correction to Oct 26 §8.** The grid fit found no common shift overnight (−16 to +21 ppm), but the zoom fits show the CH2 modes moving by up to 141 ppm. The likely reason: the grid method takes the slope of H from points ±500 Hz apart, wider than these 120–310 Hz linewidths, so it cannot follow a single mode. Its shift estimates understate mode shifts, including S1 → S2 (WORKLIST O2) and the coming S3 comparison.

**What carries over to MEMS (interpretation):**
- In linewidths, the steps moved modes by up to 0.18 and the night by up to 0.07. The undisturbed 20-minute change stays under 0.005.
- The temperature part belongs to the material, so a chip keeps it. At +40 to +90 ppm/°C, a fused-silica mode at Q = 10⁴ (100 ppm wide) moves 0.4–0.9 linewidths per °C. Holding it to 5% of a linewidth needs about 0.1°C, or a reference mode. On this bench, a common reference would have removed about half of the night's variance, mount included.
- The step part (contact, Q and coupling changes) belongs to the mount. A monolithic chip has no foam; its equivalent is package stress.
